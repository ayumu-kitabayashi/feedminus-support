Does grayscale on iPhone reduce scrolling? How to set it up, and what it doesn't change (2026)

Apple doesn't say grayscale cuts screen time. It describes Color Filters as an accessibility setting that can show the whole screen in grayscale, and we found no official source saying it makes you scroll less, so treat it as an experiment. Turn it on in Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size › Color Filters, then use the Accessibility Shortcut (triple-click the side button) or Back Tap to switch it quickly. Grayscale only changes colors. Reels, Shorts, autoplay and notifications stay as they are. If you want Reels and Shorts gone, minulo hides them for free and your DMs keep working.

Turn on grayscale

  1. Open Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size.
  2. Tap Color Filters and turn on Color Filters.
  3. Tap Grayscale.

Apple describes this as a filter that can show the entire screen in grayscale, adjust colors for color vision deficiencies, or tint the screen. Apple also notes that color filters can change the look of images and videos.

Put it on a triple-click or Back Tap

Accessibility Shortcut: go to Settings › Accessibility › Accessibility Shortcut and select the features you want. Then triple-click the side button (on iPhone with Face ID) or the Home button. Apple says that if you pick only one feature, the triple-click turns it on or off right away. If you pick several, you choose from a menu. Apple's page doesn't list every feature that can go on the shortcut, so check that Color Filters is there on your iPhone.

Back Tap: go to Settings › Accessibility › Touch › Back Tap, choose Double Tap or Triple Tap, and pick an action. Apple says Back Tap can turn on an accessibility feature or run a shortcut. To turn it off, pick None.

You can also add accessibility controls to Control Center. Apple says there are categories of controls for accessibility and vision when you tap Add a Control.

Can grayscale turn on when you open Instagram?

Apple's Shortcuts guide has an App trigger with Is Opened and Is Closed options, so an automation can run when you open or leave a chosen app. Apple lists the App trigger among the automations that can run without asking you first. What Apple's guide doesn't do is name an action that switches color filters, so we could not confirm this part. If you build it, look for a color filter action in the Shortcuts action list on your iPhone.

What grayscale doesn't change

Grayscale changes how colors are drawn on the screen. It doesn't change what apps show you. Instagram still has the Reels tab, YouTube still has Shorts, the next video still plays when you swipe, and notifications still arrive. Apple notes one thing worth knowing: Night Shift removes inverted colors and color filter effects, except grayscale. So grayscale stays on at night even when Night Shift starts.

As for whether gray makes scrolling less tempting, Apple's pages don't make that claim, and we did not find an official source that does. If you try it, look at Screen Time's report for the app before and after a week rather than going by feel.

How do I turn on grayscale on iPhone?

How do I turn grayscale on and off quickly?

Use the Accessibility Shortcut in Settings, Accessibility, Accessibility Shortcut, then triple-click the side or Home button. If Color Filters is the only feature you pick, the triple-click switches it right away. Back Tap in Settings, Accessibility, Touch, Back Tap can also turn on an accessibility feature.
