Hide Shorts with Unhook (Chrome, Firefox, Edge)

Unhook's website describes it as a way to watch YouTube without distractions, blocking suggestion feeds, comments and more, and has install buttons for Chrome, Firefox and Edge. Its Chrome Web Store listing has about 30 switches, including Hide YouTube Shorts, the Home feed, the recommended sidebar, end screen suggestions, comments, and Explore and Trending. When we looked on 2026-11-02, the listing showed 1,000,000 users, a 4.9 rating from 4.6K ratings, an update on March 22, 2026 (version 1.6.9), no price, and a request for donations.

Neither the website nor the listing mentions Safari. The only phone use described is mobile YouTube (m.youtube.com) through Firefox on Android. Other extensions on the Chrome Web Store also say they hide Shorts, but this page lists only Unhook, whose website and listing we checked. We haven't tested Unhook.

Safari and Chrome on a Mac: minulo's Mac app

minulo has a Mac version that works in Safari and Chrome. The Mac app comes first: install it from the minulo.app home page, and to use Chrome, add the Chrome extension from inside the Mac app. The Mac app is in Japanese for now.

minulo's privacy policy (in Japanese) says the Mac app reads only the address of the page open in your browser and, when that page is a way into a feed or short videos, sends you to a place you choose. The Mac app and the Chrome and Safari extensions contain no analytics, and the extensions have no code that talks to outside servers. This page doesn't cover which parts of YouTube the Mac app hides. minulo is for iPhone and Mac, with no Windows or Android version.

What YouTube's own settings can do on a computer

On youtube.com, you can click More next to the title of a section on Home, including the Shorts shelf, and choose Not interested. YouTube Help describes this as feedback that tunes your recommendations and doesn't say how long a section stays away. The other lever is watch history: YouTube says that if you turn it off and have no recent history, recommendations on the homepage are removed. Why the Shorts shelf comes back

YouTube's help calls Not interested feedback that "may be used to tune your recommendations". It doesn't promise the section stays hidden, and we could not find a stated time limit. Separately, YouTube's Shorts help lists the YouTube homepage as one of the places viewers find Shorts, along with search, Subscriptions and notifications. So the shelf is part of Home by design, and a click on Not interested lowers it rather than deleting it.

The feedback is stored in your Google Account, so these also bring the shelf back: clearing your Not interested feedback in My Activity, signing out, or using a different Google account.

How do I undo Not interested on YouTube?

Go to My Activity, choose Other Google activity, and delete YouTube 'Not interested' feedback. This clears all your Not interested and Don't recommend channel feedback at once.
