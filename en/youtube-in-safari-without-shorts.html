Does youtube.com in Safari show Shorts?

Yes. YouTube's help lists where viewers find Shorts: the Shorts tab, search results, the YouTube homepage, channel pages, Subscriptions and notifications. Moving from the app to Safari doesn't take them away. The built-in options are thinner on the website, too: YouTube's help gives the steps for the Shorts feed limit and for Show fewer Shorts in the YouTube app only, and on a computer it describes Not interested on a section of Home, which is feedback with no period.

Some people see an X on the Shorts shelf on Home. YouTube's help doesn't describe it, but SlashGear and GeekChamp say it hides the shelf for 30 days, and tbreak (April 2026) found it only on the mobile website. Some guides also suggest Request Desktop Site in Safari; we couldn't confirm in YouTube Help that this removes Shorts, and on a computer, YouTube Home has a Shorts shelf too. Hide Shorts in Safari with an extension

  1. Open Safari, tap the page menu button, then tap Manage Extensions.
  2. Search for an extension or tap Browse Extensions to look in the App Store, then tap Get.
  3. Go to Settings, tap Apps, then Safari, then Extensions. Tap the extension and turn on Allow Extension.

Nope is free with no subscriptions, in-app purchases or ads, per its App Store page. It is a Safari Web Extension for youtube.com and instagram.com, and on YouTube it hides Shorts in the sidebar, the Home shelf, the search chip and channel pages. It needs iOS 17 or later. It works only in Safari, so if the YouTube app is still on your phone, the app keeps its Shorts.

Should you delete the YouTube app?

It helps only as far as it goes. Apple's guide says deleting an app removes it and its data from your iPhone; your YouTube account stays. To delete it, touch and hold YouTube on the Home Screen, tap Remove App, then Delete App, then Delete. The catch: youtube.com still shows Shorts in Safari unless you add an extension, and you can download the app again in a minute.

If you want an icon back, open youtube.com in Safari, tap the page menu button, then Share, scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen. Open as Web App makes it open like an app. It is still the same website, Shorts included, and we couldn't confirm whether Safari extensions also run in a web app. iPhone Screen Time can block the whole YouTube app instead of deleting it, but it can't target Shorts only.
