What brain rot means

Oxford University Press chose "brain rot" as its 2024 Word of the Year after a public vote of more than 37,000 people, and said its use rose by 230% between 2023 and 2024. Its definition starts with "the supposed deterioration", which tells you it describes a feeling more than a diagnosis.

What the research says

In a 2025 meta-analysis in Psychological Bulletin, Nguyen and colleagues combined 71 studies covering 98,299 people. Heavier short video use was associated with poorer attention and inhibitory control. The review shows a link, not proof that short videos cause it. A 2025 experiment in PNAS Nexus found that blocking mobile internet on smartphones for two weeks improved sustained attention. For more, see do short videos ruin your attention span.

Is brain rot real?

It is a slang term, not a diagnosis. Oxford defines it as the supposed deterioration of a person's mental state from too much trivial online content. Studies link heavy short video use with poorer attention but haven't proven cause.

How long does it take to recover from brain rot?

There is no medical timeline for it. In one 2025 experiment, people whose mobile internet was blocked for two weeks showed better sustained attention, but that blocked all mobile internet, not only short videos.
