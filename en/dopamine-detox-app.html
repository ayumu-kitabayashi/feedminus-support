What a dopamine detox is, and isn't

Harvard Health's article (Peter Grinspoon, MD, 2020-02-26) makes two points. First, the science in the name is wrong: dopamine doesn't drop because you stop scrolling. Second, the useful part is behavioral. Sepah's version targets specific behaviors that cause you distress and limits them at set times, and the article calls that idea noble, healthy and worthwhile. Avoiding people was never part of it.

So the question for an app isn't whether it lowers dopamine. It is whether it makes one specific habit, such as opening Reels between classes, harder to start.

Remove the cues on iPhone

Move the app off the Home Screen. Touch and hold the app, tap Remove App, then Remove from Home Screen. It stays in App Library.

Hide it. Touch and hold the app, tap Require Face ID, then Hide and Require Face ID. It moves to the Hidden folder at the bottom of App Library. Apple describes this as a privacy feature, so it adds a step but doesn't stop you.

Turn off its notifications. Go to Settings › Notifications, tap the app and turn off Allow Notifications, or put it in a Scheduled Summary.

Set the hours

On iOS 26, go to Settings › Screen Time › Downtime, tap Scheduled and set your detox hours, for example evenings or one day of the weekend with Customize Days. Set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at Downtime, or Apple's default lets you keep using the apps. On iOS 27 the same idea is a Screen Time Schedule with Time Allowances.
