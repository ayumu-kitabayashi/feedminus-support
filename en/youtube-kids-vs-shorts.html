On your own account, the limit can be ignored

  1. Sign in to the YouTube app.
  2. Tap the You tab, then Settings.
  3. Tap Time management, then Shorts feed limit.
  4. Pick a time from the list. Zero is one of the choices.

YouTube's help says that when you reach the limit in the Shorts feed, a message reminds you, and you are offered the option to dismiss or ignore your set limit. So on an adult account, the limit is a nudge you can tap past.

On a supervised teen account, the parent sets it

A supervised teen account is a teen's own Google Account linked to a parent's account. YouTube describes it as voluntary: either the parent or the teen can start setup, both have to agree to link, and either one can turn supervision off at any time. YouTube says teens can also use Family Center to see their linked parent and turn supervision on or off.

Once linked, a parent can set limits on the Shorts feed, set take a break and bedtime reminders, and change the teen's content setting. To get there, YouTube's help says to sign in on a computer, click your profile picture, then Settings, scroll to Family Center and click Manage kids profiles and features for teens, then select your teen. The help pages we read don't list the time choices for teens.

When YouTube announced this on January 14, 2026, it said parents could set how much time a teen spends scrolling Shorts and that an option to set the timer to zero was coming soon. In April 2026 YouTube said the zero option is now live for all parents. Its example was zero during homework and 60 minutes on a long car trip.

What YouTube's help doesn't say

The adult help page says you can dismiss or ignore the limit. The teen pages say a parent can set the limit, but none of the pages we read say whether the teen sees the same dismiss option or not. We couldn't confirm either way, so don't count on a parent-set limit being impossible to skip. Also, because a teen can turn supervision off, the limit holds only as long as both keep the accounts linked.

Kid accounts and YouTube Kids are different things

A supervised kid account is a parent-managed version of regular YouTube for children under 13. YouTube's comparison page says parents can use Family Center to manage screen time limits for it, including a Shorts feed limit. Some features are turned off for these accounts, and making Shorts is one of them.

YouTube Kids is a separate app. YouTube says screen time there is set with a built-in timer: a parent opens Settings, solves a multiplication problem or enters a passcode, taps Set Timer and starts it. When time is up, the app locks until a parent unlocks it. That timer covers the whole app, not just short videos.

Can I ignore the YouTube Shorts feed limit?

On your own account, yes. YouTube's help says that when you reach the limit, a reminder appears and you can dismiss or ignore your set limit.

Can parents set a Shorts limit on a teen's YouTube account?

Yes, with a supervised teen account. A linked parent can set limits on the Shorts feed in Family Center, along with take a break and bedtime reminders and the content setting. Supervision is voluntary, and either the parent or the teen can turn it off.

Can a teen skip a Shorts limit set by a parent?

YouTube's help doesn't say. The adult page says the limit can be dismissed or ignored, and the teen pages say only that parents can set it, so we could not confirm whether teens see a dismiss option.

Does a supervised kid account have a Shorts limit?

YouTube's comparison page says parents can use Family Center to manage screen time limits for supervised kid accounts, including setting a Shorts feed limit. Making Shorts is among the features turned off for these accounts.

How do time limits work in YouTube Kids?

YouTube Kids has a built-in timer. A parent opens Settings, solves a multiplication problem or enters a passcode, taps Set Timer and starts it. When time is up, kids see a Time's up! notification and the app locks.
