When a Screen Time limit for Instagram doesn't stop you, there are five usual causes: limits can be ignored by default, you are opening instagram.com in Safari, iOS 27 changed how limits work, devices in Family Sharing are not up to date, or Instagram is on the Always Allowed list. Why is my Instagram Screen Time limit not working?

Most often because Screen Time limits can be ignored by default. If you only want Reels gone and DMs kept, minulo is another option (hiding is free). Set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at End of Limit so the app can't be used without the passcode. Also check Safari (instagram.com), Always Allowed and, on iOS 27, Time Allowances.

Why can I still open Instagram in Safari?

An app limit applies to the app. On iOS 26, add instagram.com under Never Allow in Content & Privacy Restrictions > Web Content. On iOS 27, a Time Allowance also covers a website the app declares as associated.

Can Screen Time limit only Instagram Reels?

No. Screen Time limits apply to whole apps (categories on iOS 27), and website blocking applies to whole addresses. To stop only Reels, use an app that hides them.

Why don't Screen Time limits work on my child's iPhone?

Update every device in your Family Sharing group to the latest software and set Screen Time from the parent's device. The new iOS 27 controls only work when every device in the group runs iOS 27 or later.

1. Turn on Block at Downtime (iOS 26)

Apple notes that by default, Screen Time limits can be ignored once they are reached. For Downtime this means: with Block at Downtime off, you see a reminder that it's downtime, but you can choose to keep using the app. With it on, restricted apps can't be opened during downtime. They appear dimmed with an hourglass icon.

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time.
  2. If you have no passcode yet, scroll down, tap Lock Screen Time Settings, and enter a four-digit passcode twice. On iOS 27, tap Screen Time > Passcode & Management > Lock Screen Time Settings.
  3. Tap Downtime, then turn on Block at Downtime. Apple's iOS 27 user guide doesn't list this option.

Apple says you choose between blocked and dimmed once you have set a Screen Time passcode, so if you don't see the switch, set the passcode first.

5. On iOS 27: Screen Time Schedules

In Apple's iOS 27 guide, the page on Screen Time schedules no longer covers Downtime. It covers Screen Time Schedules and Time Allowances instead. You go to Screen Time › Screen Time Schedule, turn on Schedule, tap Add Schedule, and choose which Time Allowance categories, such as Social Media, are available in each time window.

Apple says these features need iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, watchOS 27, visionOS 27 or later on all your Apple devices, and in a Family Sharing group every device must be upgraded. If an iPad or Mac is still on an older version, update it. Always Allowed still applies here: apps on it stay available regardless of schedules and allowances.
