Hide the feed, block the app, or delete it? What actually sticks (2026)

There are four ways to cut back on Instagram and similar apps: delete the app, block the whole app (Screen Time, Opal, Brick), add a pause before it opens (one sec, ScreenZen), or hide only the feed (minulo, SocialLite, UNDOOMED). Deleting and blocking are the strongest but take DMs with them, so many people reinstall or unblock to answer a message. A pause works best for opening apps on autopilot, but after it the full app opens. Hiding Reels keeps DMs and is the easiest to live with, but only inside that app, so pair it with a lock on the official app.

Deleting the app: strong, but it rarely stays deleted

Deleting Instagram before exams is a common move, and it works for as long as you don't need it. The trouble is that Instagram is also where club announcements, a group chat or a friend's message arrive. When one of those comes in, or exams end, the app goes back on and the feed is exactly where you left it. Deleting also doesn't cover the browser: instagram.com still opens in Safari.

It fits best when the break has a clear end and you have another way to reach the people who matter, or for an app you never use for messages.

Blocking the whole app: the passcode decides

Apple says that by default Screen Time limits can be ignored once reached. To make Downtime stick, you set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at Downtime, so blocked apps appear dimmed with an hourglass and can't be opened. If you set that passcode yourself, you know it, and the block lasts until the first time you really want in. Asking a friend or family member to choose it is the simple fix.

Opal blocks apps and websites on a schedule and has a Deep Focus mode that can't be cancelled. Brick needs a physical device to unblock. These are stronger, but all of them block DMs along with the feed. If you open the app to answer one message, you are back on the feed.

Adding a pause: good for autopilot, not for Reels

one sec shows a short pause, such as a breathing animation, when you open an app you chose, and it is free for one app. ScreenZen adds a wait that can grow each time, interrupts scrolling and can set strict blocks, and it is free and donation-supported. Both work on any app, not only social media.

A pause catches the times you open Instagram without meaning to. When you do mean to, you wait a few seconds and then get the full app, Reels and all.

Hiding only the feed: keeps DMs, but only inside that app

minulo, SocialLite and UNDOOMED open social networks in their own view and remove Reels, short-video feeds and recommendations, while DMs keep working. You can still reply to the group chat without the feed waiting for you.

The limit is that no iPhone app can change the official Instagram app. If the official icon is still one tap away, habit takes you there. minulo handles this by locking the official Instagram, TikTok and YouTube apps with Screen Time, so tapping the usual icon shows a block screen and you use minulo instead. Hiding is free on all 10 networks it supports, and a free block lasts up to 2 hours (you can start a new one right away). Schedules, longer blocks, Hard Mode and multiple block lists are Pro. While an official app is blocked, its notifications don't arrive, and minulo sends none of its own. Some features missing from the web versions, such as posting Stories, voice messages and calls, don't work in minulo.

What the research says

  • A pause: In a 6-week field experiment with 280 participants, "on average 36% of the times participants attempted opening a target app, they closed that app again after one sec interfered", and the app "decreased users' actual opening of target apps by 57% after six consecutive weeks." A follow-up online experiment (N = 500) found that the option to dismiss had the strongest effect, the time delay also helped, and the deliberation message was not effective. One author is the originator of one sec (Grüning, Riedel and Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS, 2023).
  • Quitting for a while: In a randomized experiment, deactivating Facebook for four weeks before the 2018 US midterm election increased subjective well-being and offline time with family and friends, and "caused a large persistent reduction in post-experiment Facebook use" (Allcott, Braghieri, Eichmeyer and Gentzkow, NBER working paper, 2019).
  • The cost of cutting off friends: 138 active Facebook users were asked either to give it up for 5 days or keep using it. Those who gave it up had lower cortisol and also lower life satisfaction (Vanman, Baker and Tobin, Journal of Social Psychology, 2018).

These studies are about Facebook or about apps in general, not about Instagram Reels, and we found no peer-reviewed study that tests hiding only the feed. In minulo's own anonymous data, people who opened a social network inside minulo 10 or more times on their first day came back on a later day 73% of the time, against 10% for people who opened none. That shows who stays, not what causes it, but it fits the idea that keeping the parts you use matters.

Which one should you pick?

  • You open Instagram for messages and lose an hour to Reels: hide the feed and lock the official app.
  • You open lots of apps without thinking: add a pause with one sec or ScreenZen.
  • You want a few hours with nothing at all: Downtime with Block at Downtime, with someone else holding the passcode, or Opal.
  • You don't need the app for anyone: delete it, and block instagram.com in Safari too.

They also combine well. For example, a pause on games and YouTube, a Reels-free Instagram for messages, and Downtime at night.

Should I delete Instagram or just block Reels?

If you need Instagram for messages, hiding only Reels usually lasts longer than deleting, because you never have to reinstall it to answer a DM. Deleting fits a short, clear period such as exam week, or an app you don't use for messages. You can also do both halfway: hide Reels with an app like minulo and lock the official Instagram app with Screen Time.

Why don't app blockers work for me?

Usually for one of three reasons. Screen Time limits can be ignored by default unless you set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at Downtime. If you chose the passcode yourself, you can type it in. And a full block also stops DMs, so you unblock to reply and then stay on the feed. Letting someone else set the passcode, or keeping DMs in an app without Reels, fixes most of this.

Does a pause before opening an app actually work?

There is evidence that it helps. In a 6-week field experiment with 280 one sec users published in PNAS in 2023, people closed the app 36% of the times one sec interrupted them, and opening of the chosen apps fell by 57% after six weeks. One of the authors is one sec's creator. After the pause the whole app opens, so if you do go in, Reels are still there.

Can I keep Instagram DMs but get rid of Reels?

Not inside the official Instagram app, which no other iPhone app can change. Apps such as minulo, SocialLite and UNDOOMED open Instagram in their own view with Reels and suggested posts hidden while DMs keep working. In minulo this is free, and it can also lock the official Instagram app so you end up in the version without Reels.

Is hiding the feed enough on its own?

Often not, because the official app is still on your phone with Reels in it. Hiding only works inside the app that does the hiding. Pair it with a lock on the official app (minulo does this with Screen Time, free for up to 2 hours per block) or move the official app off your Home Screen.
